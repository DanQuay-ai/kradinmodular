What’s your Kradin?

Find a day

The Akan day begins at daybreak, not midnight. A birth in the small hours belongs to the day before.

Kradin, the soul name

Kra is the soul, din the name. Every Akan person carries the name of the weekday they were born on, and with it the ɔkra of that day: one of seven souls of the Creator, Onyankopɔn. Kwasi and Akosua belong to Ayisi on Sunday, Kwadwo and Adwoa to Adwo on Monday, and so on through the week.

The kradin was never only a label. It carried a temperament, praise names, taboos and a day of observance, and it placed each person inside the 42-day adaduanan that ordered Akan life.

Kɔradin, the script

Kɔradin is a syllabary for Twi that takes its name from kradin, spelled the way Twi says it. Each sign writes a syllable, built from a radical and its modifiers, so a name is written as it is spoken.

Every name on this page is shown in Kɔradin above its Latin form. Hover or tap any Kɔradin to read it. Try the script yourself in the type tester.

Sacred days this year

How it is counted

  • Adaduanan. Six named prefixes (fo, nwona, nkyi, kuru, kwa, mono) turn against the seven weekday stems. They meet again after 42 days, and each of those days has its own name: the nnanson. Day 1 here is Fodwoɔ, a Monday, as in Takyiman and Kwawu; in Kumase the count is opened at Nwonawukuo, the same cycle read from another day.
  • Sacred days. Awukudae (Kuruwukuo) on day 10, Fofie on day 19, Akwasidae (Kurukwasi) on day 28. Each day is also dapaa, dabɔne or dahunu, after Konadu 2012.
  • Anchor. Adommerɛ began on Monday 21 July 2025. The count runs unbroken in both directions: every Akwasidae reported below lands on Kurukwasi, and all eight dated 19th-century Asante events in Konadu 2012 land on the day he names.
  • Year (afe). Nine adaduanan, 378 days. One is dropped every three years so that Odwira stays between August and September (Danquah, cited by Konadu). Here the year opens with the adaduanan starting nearest 10 August: 9, 9, 8 in turn. In a year of 8, ɔpɛnimaa is the one left out.
  • Names. The nine seasonal periods follow Konadu's Table 5. He notes these "months" were never fixed, so the sequence here is a standardisation.
  • Akwasidae Kɛseɛ (✦) is called by the Asantehene, not fixed by the count, so only the recorded ones are marked.
