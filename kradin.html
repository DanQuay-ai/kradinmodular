Kradin, the soul name

Kra is the soul, din the name. Every Akan person carries the name of the weekday they were born on, and with it the ɔkra of that day: one of the seven souls of the Creator. Kwasi and Akosua belong to Ayisi on Sunday, Kwadwo and Adwoa to Adwo on Monday, and so on through the week.

A kradin is more than a label. It carries a character, praise names and observances, and places each person inside the 42-day adaduanan that ordered Akan life.

Kɔradin, the script

Kɔradin is a syllabary for Twi. Each sign writes a syllable, built from a radical and its modifiers, so a name is written as it is spoken.

The Kɔradin writing system

A syllabary for Twi, built so that Akan words are written the way they are spoken, one sign per syllable.

Origins

Kɔradin was created in 2021, one of the first creations of Merankron. It takes its name from kradin, the soul name, spelled as Twi pronounces it.

West Africa already writes with symbols. Akan, Ga and Ewe signs have long carried ideas, proverbs and values rather than sounds, Adinkra being the best known. Such ideographic signs are shared across neighbouring peoples, not owned by one. Kɔradin draws on that inheritance, with Afaka, Adinkra, and Ewe and Ga symbols as references, and turns it into a phonetic script: every sign now stands for a sound.

Ambition

Latin letters were brought to Twi by missionaries and administrators, and each spelled Akan names their own way: Kwasi, Quassi, Kouassi, Kwesi. Kɔradin gives Twi a script made for its own sounds, so a word has one spelling wherever it is written.

  • Everyday use. A font family, a keyboard, and a browser extension that shows any Twi on the web in Kɔradin.
  • Learning. Mnemonics and short lessons, so anyone can read their own name in an afternoon.
  • Recognition. Documentation of the script towards registration and, in time, Unicode encoding.
  • Design. A visual language for Neo-Akan art, fashion, architecture and media.

How it works

One sign, one syllable.

Twi is built from consonant and vowel pairs. Each pair is one sign: ka, kɔ, kye, twe.

Families of signs.

Related sounds share a base sign. Variants are that base with a modifier: ky and tw grow from t, dw and gy from d, b and kp from p.

Seven vowels.

a, e, ɛ, i, o, ɔ, u. The open vowels ɛ and ɔ have their own forms, never mixed with e and o.

Nasals as marks.

m, n and ng become marks: under the sign they close, or before the sign they open (ka, kan, kang).

No clusters.

Consonant clusters take their vowel back: Brafo is written barafo, Frimpɔŋ firimpɔŋ.

Phonetic names.

Names are spelled as said: Mensah mɛnsa, Dontoh dontɔ, Kwesi with its own kwe sign.
