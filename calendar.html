Akan calendar

Forty-two days make one adaduanan: the six-day and the seven-day weeks meeting again. Eight or nine of them make a year. Four days in each are sacred, for the ancestors and the abosom: no farming or heavy work on these days. Hover or tap any Kɔradin to read it in Latin.

How it is counted

  • Adaduanan. 42 days, always starting on a Dwoada (Monday) with Fɔdwoo. Then every nine days: Awukudae on day 10 (a Wukuada), Fofie on day 19 (a Fiada), Akwasidae on day 28 (a Kwasiada).
  • Anchor. Adommerɛ began on Monday 21 July 2025 (your calendar graphics). The count runs unbroken in both directions, and every Akwasidae reported below lands on day 28.
  • Year. 9 adaduanan are 378 days and 8 are 336, so neither matches the sun. A year opens with the adaduanan that starts nearest 28 July: two years in three have 9, the third has 8. That puts Adommerɛ's Akwasidae between late July and mid September, and it lands exactly on the 2026 Akwasidae Kɛseɛ (30 August).
  • The ninth. Your pages name eight adaduanan. The ninth, in years that have one, is shown as Odwira for now: tell me its name and it changes everywhere.
  • Akwasidae Kɛseɛ (✦) is called by the Asantehene, not fixed by the count, so only the recorded ones are marked.
